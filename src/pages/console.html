How the model works

From raw readings to a position you can trust.

Ingestrange · RSSI · anchor map
Qualifyscore every reading
Estimatelearned model + filter
Reportposition · uncertainty · reason

1Ingest

Each anchor that hears a tag reports a timestamped range (ultra-wideband time of flight) or signal strength (Bluetooth RSSI). With the surveyed anchor coordinates and the floor plan, the system knows which wall sits between every anchor and every point.

Works through measurement adapters, so anchor vendors can be mixed.

2Qualify

A radio path that passes through a wall reads longer than the real distance. Each reading therefore gets a trust score from three checks: how far it disagrees with neighbouring anchors, whether the straight path crosses a wall on the plan, and whether it fits recent motion. Low-trust readings are down-weighted rather than thrown away, so a sparse site keeps every clue it has.

This is why the live map draws solid rays for trusted anchors and dashed rays for down-weighted ones.

3Estimate

A learned model turns the weighted readings into an X–Y position. A temporal filter then blends in motion history, which smooths jitter and lets the track continue when anchors drop out; during a gap the filter predicts forward and the uncertainty grows honestly. Location-specific corrections from the error map are applied last.

Model family and filter settings are tuned per site during the pilot.

4Report

Every position leaves with an uncertainty radius, a confidence value, the anchors that influenced it and a plain-language reason. Operators read “2 of 8 anchors blocked, track held by motion history” instead of a bare coordinate.

Reports and events can be exported to your own systems.

What it uses

Inputs, model parts and outputs.

Inputs

Range
Ultra-wideband time-of-flight distance from each reachable anchor.
Signal strength
Bluetooth RSSI where UWB is not installed.
Anchor coordinates
Surveyed X–Y positions and anchor identifiers.
Floor plan
Walls and doors, used for line-of-sight checks and placement planning.
Device identifier
A rotating pseudonymous ID, never a name.

Model parts

Reading scorer
Estimates how likely each reading is obstructed or biased.
Position estimator
Learned mapping from trusted readings to X–Y.
Temporal filter
Uses motion history for stability, prediction and recovery.
Uncertainty estimate
Produces the radius and confidence shown with every position.
Error-map corrector
Learns repeat errors by location and checks them on separate trajectories.
Planner and stress tester
Simulate placements and thousands of noise, dropout and obstruction cases.

Outputs

Position
X–Y in metres with zone name.
Uncertainty
Radius around the estimate, plus confidence.
Evidence
Anchors used, anchors down-weighted, and the reason in plain language.
Events
Dropouts, uncertain zones, emergency flags and audit entries.
Reports
Placement comparison, error-map validation and stress-test findings.
Where it is necessary

Wherever a few metres change the outcome.

  • Hospitals. Wheelchairs, pumps and monitors sit in busy corridors behind many walls; finding the right one quickly saves staff minutes, and emergency mode keeps priority assets visible.
  • Warehouses. Metal racking bends signals, so a pallet can appear one aisle away; obstruction scoring and the error map target exactly this.
  • Campuses and public buildings. Large floors with few budgeted anchors need wayfinding and space planning without a dense install.
  • Malls. Crowds change signal quality through the day; crowd-aware zones show where accuracy dips.
